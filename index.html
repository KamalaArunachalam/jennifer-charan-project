Her story

Jennifer before Charan: A life in many places

I've lived in enough places to know that home isn't always a straightforward answer to the question, "Where are you from?" Assam is where my roots lie, but Bengal, Odisha, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and the many places I've called home have all shaped me in different ways. And then there's Calcutta, the city where I spent much of my growing-up years, and one I will always carry a little differently.

I have a soft spot for Calcutta. Its old neighbourhoods, crumbling buildings, bookshops, cafés, crowded streets, yellow taxis, and the Hooghly flowing quietly alongside it all. I love the city not because it's perfect, but because it has never needed to be. There's history in its walls, stories in its streets, and a certain stubbornness in the way it continues to be itself. Calcutta gave me memories, shaped my sensibilities, and became a part of who I am. No matter how many places I live in, a part of me will always find its way back to it.

I've studied, worked, moved cities, started over, and built a life of my own. Today, I'm an academic who teaches journalism and media, and social and strategic communication. I spend much of my professional life thinking about stories, who gets to tell them, and how they shape the way we see the world.

I seem to have inherited my father's work ethic and my mother's love for wandering. I can work myself into the ground when something matters to me, but I'm just as happy loitering around a city, discovering unfamiliar streets, listening to music, or watching a film.

I'm curious, opinionated, and not particularly good at accepting things simply because they've always been done a certain way. My politics and worldview stem from a simple conviction: everyone deserves the freedom to live on their own terms, provided they extend the same freedom to others.

Years of living independently have taught me to stand on my own, start again when necessary, and make a home wherever life takes me. They've also taught me that independence doesn't mean having to do everything alone.

I love rivers and seas, cats (and almost all other furry creatures), aloofness, and cities that have stories to tell. Cinema and music occupy a ridiculous amount of space in my life. I have restless thoughts, unfinished plans, contradictions, and days when I have absolutely no idea what I'm doing. I'm still figuring things out, and I've made peace with that.

My life hasn't followed a straight line, and I don't suppose it ever will. I've spent much of it figuring out who I am, what I believe in, and the kind of life I want to live.

And somewhere along the way, I met someone with whom I wanted to share it.

Not because my story needed completing, but because I found someone I wanted beside me for whatever comes next.

His story

A love that feels like home

Born in Bangalore, my early life was defined by early challenges that taught me to look inward. Guided by my mother's quiet strength and unwavering belief in me, I learned to navigate a world not built for my mind, becoming an observer who read the subtleties of people and environments. This perceptiveness born out of necessity ultimately became my guiding compass.

My foundation was also shaped by a childhood of blunt candor. Rather than accumulating a crowd, I found my anchor in one, enduring friendship. That bond has been my anchor at times, and a sail for me in others. I learned to find profound joy in the small things: in endless self-education, in solitude, and in expressing my opinions without needing the validation of grand gestures.

Recently, I stepped into the world of education. My career so far has given me the tools to teach them what they needed to know, and my experience in life gave me the tools to put it across in a way they would understand.

Then came Jennifer. Her world and mine were different, yet our connection wasn't defined by those differences, but by how they complement one another. My grounded resilience meets her brilliant, intelligent world, one shaped by a sheltered upbringing, yet radiating warmth and wonder, creating a partnership built on mutual respect.

This is my journey, one of observation, enduring loyalty, and finding a deep, meaningful love that feels like home.
