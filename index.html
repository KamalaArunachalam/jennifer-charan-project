Her story

Jennifer before Charan: A life in many places

I've lived in enough places to know that home isn't always a straightforward answer to the question, "Where are you from?" Assam is where my roots lie, but Bengal, Odisha, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and the many places I've called home have all shaped me in different ways. And then there's Calcutta, the city where I spent much of my growing-up years, and one I will always carry a little differently.

I have a soft spot for Calcutta. Its old neighbourhoods, crumbling buildings, bookshops, cafés, crowded streets, yellow taxis, and the Hooghly flowing quietly alongside it all. I love the city not because it's perfect, but because it has never needed to be. There's history in its walls, stories in its streets, and a certain stubbornness in the way it continues to be itself. Calcutta gave me memories, shaped my sensibilities, and became a part of who I am. No matter how many places I live in, a part of me will always find its way back to it.

I've studied, worked, moved cities, started over, and built a life of my own. Today, I'm an academic who teaches journalism and media, and social and strategic communication. I spend much of my professional life thinking about stories, who gets to tell them, and how they shape the way we see the world.

I seem to have inherited my father's work ethic and my mother's love for wandering. I can work myself into the ground when something matters to me, but I'm just as happy loitering around a city, discovering unfamiliar streets, listening to music, or watching a film.

I'm curious, opinionated, and not particularly good at accepting things simply because they've always been done a certain way. My politics and worldview stem from a simple conviction: everyone deserves the freedom to live on their own terms, provided they extend the same freedom to others.

Years of living independently have taught me to stand on my own, start again when necessary, and make a home wherever life takes me.

They've also taught me that independence doesn't mean having to do everything alone.

I love rivers and seas, cats (and almost all other furry creatures), aloofness, and cities that have stories to tell. Cinema and music occupy a ridiculous amount of space in my life. I have restless thoughts, unfinished plans, contradictions, and days when I have absolutely no idea what I'm doing. I'm still figuring things out, and I've made peace with that.

My life hasn't followed a straight line, and I don't suppose it ever will. I've spent much of it figuring out who I am, what I believe in, and the kind of life I want to live.

And somewhere along the way, I met Charan.

We came from different places, spoke different languages, grew up with different cultural and religious influences, and carried very different ideas about certain things. Yet, somewhere between conversations, shared meals, films, music, wandering around cities, and the ordinary little rituals of everyday life, we found our way to each other.

What I appreciate most about us is that neither of us had to become a different person for the other. We have our differences, our disagreements, our peculiarities, and our own ways of looking at the world. But we've also found a way to make room for each other. He has brought a certain steadiness into my otherwise restless life, and I've found companionship in someone with whom even the most ordinary days can become memorable.

I never particularly imagined myself getting married. The institution, the traditions, and much of the social expectation surrounding it were never things I felt especially drawn to. But meeting Charan made me reconsider what marriage could mean when it is a choice made between two people, rather than an obligation dictated by everything around them. Our decision to build a life together is, in many ways, an extension of the lives we've already lived: independent, individual, imperfect, and entirely our own.

I'm not looking for someone to complete my story. I've lived too much of it, in too many places, to believe that I was ever incomplete. I'm simply glad to have found someone with whom I want to write the next chapters.

And perhaps that's what home will continue to mean to me: not necessarily a city, a language, a culture, or even a place, but the freedom to be entirely myself, wherever life takes me, and the joy of having someone beside me who understands that.

His story

A love that feels like home

Born in Bangalore, my early life was defined by early challenges that taught me to look inward. Guided by my mother's quiet strength and unwavering belief in me, I learned to navigate a world not built for my mind, becoming an observer who read the subtleties of people and environments. This perceptiveness born out of necessity ultimately became my guiding compass.

My foundation was also shaped by a childhood of blunt candor. Rather than accumulating a crowd, I found my anchor in one, enduring friendship. That bond has been my anchor at times, and a sail for me in others. I learned to find profound joy in the small things: in endless self-education, in solitude, and in expressing my opinions without needing the validation of grand gestures.

Recently, I stepped into the world of education. My career so far has given me the tools to teach them what they needed to know, and my experience in life gave me the tools to put it across in a way they would understand.

Then came Jennifer. Her world and mine were different, yet our connection wasn't defined by those differences, but by how they complement one another. My grounded resilience meets her brilliant, intelligent world, one shaped by a sheltered upbringing, yet radiating warmth and wonder, creating a partnership built on mutual respect.

This is my journey, one of observation, enduring loyalty, and finding a deep, meaningful love that feels like home.

Our story

Happily-ever-onward

It started at work in May 2024. Neither of us knew that an ordinary workplace acquaintance would eventually turn into our most important collaborative project. There was no grand cinematic moment, no carefully written script, and certainly no indication that we were signing up for a lifetime of each other's company.

But somewhere between conversations that ran a little longer than necessary, shared meals, city explorations and finding increasingly more reasons to spend time together, something began to take shape.

By January 2025, we had decided to give this relationship a name.

We came from different places, spoke different languages, grew up with different cultural references and had our own ways of looking at the world. On paper, we had enough differences to keep a comparison chart busy. In practice, we discovered that we didn't need to agree on everything to get along, and certainly didn't need to become the same person to build something meaningful together.

What followed was a rather lovely collection of ordinary and extraordinary moments. Discovering each other's worlds, introducing each other to familiar foods, films and music, exploring cities, exchanging cultural references, sharing coffee, Sunday dosas and long conversations about just about everything. There have been new experiences, old habits, occasional disagreements, plenty of laughter and the ongoing discovery that two people can approach the same situation in entirely different ways and still find their way to each other.

Over time, we became part of each other's routines, plans and imagined futures. We learnt that companionship isn't always about grand gestures or perfectly timed declarations. Sometimes it is about showing up, making room for each other's individuality, remembering the little things, looking after one another and having someone with whom even the most uneventful day feels worth sharing.

We've grown individually and together. We've learnt to navigate differences without having to erase them, to make compromises without losing ourselves, and to understand that building a life together is less about having everything figured out and more about being willing to figure things out together.

And somewhere along the way, marriage stopped being an abstract idea and became something we genuinely wanted for ourselves. Not because we had arrived at some perfect version of life, but because we found someone with whom we wanted to take on all its unpredictability.

So, here we are.

On November 20, 2026, we're making it official. What began as an ordinary meeting has turned into a shared future, and what was once an unexpected plot twist is now a decision we're making wholeheartedly.

This isn't quite a happily-ever-after. We prefer to think of it as happily-ever-onward.

No final submission date, no fixed syllabus, no promise that everything will go according to plan. Just two people, one shared future, plenty of stories still to tell, and a lifetime of learning along the way.

We're glad you're here to celebrate the next chapter with us. After all, every good story deserves a few familiar faces in the audience.
